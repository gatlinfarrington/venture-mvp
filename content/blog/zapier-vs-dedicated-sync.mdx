Zapier is excellent at moving **new** events into Google Sheets. Many indie sellers start there: Gumroad sale → new row, Lemon Squeezy order → new row, Slack ping on refund. That pattern works until you need last year's history, stable columns after a refund, or an MRR number that matches your diligence sheet.

This post compares append-only automation to a dedicated revenue importer. Pricing and task facts below are taken from Zapier's public pages and dated so they can be re-checked. RevenueSheet is not available to install yet and is **not** claimed as installable today — [join the launch list](/#launch) for when Free and paid sync open.

## What Zapier is optimized for

Zapier connects thousands of apps with Zaps: a trigger fires, actions run, and successful action steps consume **tasks**. Zapier's own explainer: a task is counted when an action successfully completes ([What is a task in Zapier?](https://zapier.com/blog/what-is-a-task-in-zapier/); checked Oct 13, 2026).

For storefront → Sheets, a typical Zap looks like:

1. Trigger: new sale / new order / new subscriber on the storefront app
2. Action: Create Spreadsheet Row in Google Sheets
3. Optional: Formatter, Filter, or a Slack notify step

That is the right tool when you care about **forward-looking** rows and already own the formulas.

## Where append-only Zaps leave revenue sheets short

- **History.** A Zap started today does not backfill last quarter unless you build a separate one-off job. Dedicated importers walk list APIs once and keep tabs current.
- **Refunds and updates.** Append-only flows usually add a new row; updating the original sale (or netting refunds) needs extra Zaps and matching logic.
- **MRR / churn.** Zapier will not build an MRR dashboard tab for you. You still normalize intervals and statuses — see [MRR across Gumroad, Polar and Lemon Squeezy](/blog/mrr-across-gumroad-polar-lemon-squeezy).
- **Task metering.** Every successful action step is a task. Multi-step Zaps multiply usage on busy launch days.

## When Zapier still fits better

- Cross-app workflows beyond revenue (CRM updates, form → sheet → Slack, Notion mirrors)
- You only need brand-new events and will maintain Sheets formulas yourself
- You already live in Zapier for the rest of the stack and task volume is predictable

See also our fair table: [RevenueSheet vs Zapier](/vs/zapier).

## When a dedicated sheet sync fits better

- You need **full history** in stable columns, not only events after Zap activation
- Refunds should update (or clearly net) the original sale
- You want an MRR / churn dashboard tab without designing a Zap per event type
- You sell on more than one storefront and want one ledger shape

That is the job RevenueSheet is built for at launch: backfill Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Gumroad, or Stripe into tabs you own, then build the MRR tab from those rows. Free is planned for one source with manual refresh; once paid plans open, Pro adds scheduled refresh (hourly, every 6 hours, daily, or weekly) across sources (one store per source).
