# Revenue by Product: A Sheets Report for Indie Sellers

Once you have more than a couple of products, "how much did I make?" turns into "which product made it?" Each platform's dashboard answers that for its own store. If you sell in two or three places, nobody adds them up for you, and a product sold on both Gumroad and Lemon Squeezy shows up as two half-numbers.

This guide builds a revenue-by-product report in Google Sheets with one formula.

## Gross vs. net revenue by product

Decide first which number you want:

- **Gross revenue** is every completed order, before refunds.
- **Net revenue** leaves out refunded and charged-back orders.

Net is the one to make decisions with. A product that sells well but gets refunded often looks very different once refunds come out.

## Start from one Orders table

Put every order from every store in one tab called `Orders`, with the same columns no matter where the row came from:

| Column | Header | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | Order ID | 10423 |
| B | Created | 2026-09-14 |
| C | Source | Gumroad |
| D | Product | Notion budget template |
| E | Email | sam@example.com |
| F | Amount (dollars) | 19 |
| G | Status | paid |

Use a single currency for the report, or add a column for it and filter on it. Don't add dollars and euros together.

## The QUERY formula

In a new tab, this gives net revenue per product, largest first:

```
=QUERY(Orders!A:G,
  "select D, sum(F), count(A)
   where G <> 'refunded' and G <> 'chargeback' and G <> 'failed'
   group by D
   order by sum(F) desc
   label D 'Product', sum(F) 'Net revenue', count(A) 'Orders'", 1)
```

The status values are whatever your stores use. Check the distinct values first with `=UNIQUE(Orders!G2:G)` and adjust the `where` clause.

To limit it to one month, add a date condition, for example `and B >= date '2026-09-01' and B < date '2026-10-01'`.

For a split by store as well, group by both columns: `select D, C, sum(F) ... group by D, C`.

## Matching product names across stores

If you sell the same product in two places, it probably has two slightly different names. Fix that with a small mapping tab called `Products`:

| A: Name in store | B: Report name |
|---|---|
| Budget Template (Notion) | Notion budget template |
| notion-budget-template | Notion budget template |

Add a column H to `Orders` with:

```
=IFERROR(VLOOKUP(D2, Products!A:B, 2, FALSE), D2)
```

Then group by H instead of D in the QUERY. Any name you haven't mapped falls through unchanged, so nothing disappears from the report.

## Reading the result

A worked example on example data: a template at $1,240 net, a course at $860, and nine small products adding up to $310. Two things to look at:

- **The top of the list.** Those products pay the bills. They're where a price test or a better landing page matters most.
- **The long tail.** Small products aren't automatically worth cutting. Check the order count next to the revenue: lots of small orders can mean a good entry product that leads to bigger purchases.

Re-run the report monthly and keep the old tabs. The trend tells you more than any single month.
