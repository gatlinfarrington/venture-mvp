Gumroad is great at telling you what sold today. If you run memberships, though, the number you usually want is different: how much recurring revenue comes in each month. That's MRR, and getting it from Gumroad takes a little extra math, because one membership can renew monthly while another renews once a year.

This guide does it by hand in Google Sheets. At the end there's a short note on doing it automatically.

## Why Gumroad needs extra math

MRR is the revenue you can expect every month from active subscriptions. A monthly membership counts at its price. A longer billing period has to be divided down to a month: a $120 yearly membership is $10 of MRR, not $120 in the month it renews.

Here is the divisor for each billing period:

| Billing period | Gumroad label | Monthly MRR |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | `monthly` | amount as billed |
| Quarterly | `quarterly` | amount ÷ 3 |
| Every 6 months | `biannually` | amount ÷ 6 |
| Yearly | `yearly` | amount ÷ 12 |
| Every 2 years | `every_two_years` | amount ÷ 24 |

One-time sales (an ebook, a template pack) are revenue, but they aren't recurring. Keep them out of MRR and track them on their own line.

## Where the data lives

You need one row per membership, with the customer, product, status, price, and billing period. Gumroad's API returns subscribers per membership product (`GET https://api.gumroad.com/v2/products/:id/subscribers`), so you call it once per product. Each subscriber comes back with a `recurrence` (one of the labels above) and a `status`. You need an access token: in your Gumroad settings (advanced settings), create an application, and click **Generate access token**. Treat the token like a password.

The API returns money in cents ([Gumroad sale `price`](https://gumroad.com/api#sales)). A $9.00 membership comes back as `900`, so divide by 100 when you write it to the sheet.

Put the rows in a tab called `Members`:

| Column | Header | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | Email | sam@example.com |
| B | Product | Pattern club |
| C | Status | alive |
| D | Price (dollars, per billing period) | 30 |
| E | Recurrence | quarterly |
| F | Ended | (blank if still running) |

## The normalize-to-monthly formula

Add a column G called `Monthly` and put this in G2, then fill down:

```
=IF(AND(C2<>"alive", C2<>"pending_cancellation"), 0,
  D2 / SWITCH(LOWER(E2),
    "monthly", 1,
    "quarterly", 3,
    "biannually", 6,
    "yearly", 12,
    "every_two_years", 24,
    NA()))
```

The `NA()` at the end is on purpose. If a billing period shows up that the formula doesn't know, you'll see an error instead of a silently wrong number.

Your membership MRR is then:

```
=SUM(Members!G:G)
```

A worked example on example data: 30 members at $10 a month ($300), 8 at $30 a quarter ($80), and 5 at $96 a year ($40). Membership MRR is **$420**.

## Canceled vs. ended vs. failed payment

Status is where homemade MRR numbers drift. Gumroad's subscriber statuses are `alive`, `payment_method_update_required`, `pending_cancellation`, `pending_failure`, `failed_payment`, `fixed_subscription_period_ended` and `cancelled` ([Gumroad subscribers API](https://gumroad.com/api#subscribers)). Three rules keep it honest:

- **A membership stops counting on the day access ends,** not the day someone clicks cancel. `pending_cancellation` means the member asked to cancel but still has access, so the formula above keeps it in MRR until the subscription actually ends.
- **Failed payments are a choice.** `pending_failure` means the latest charge failed and Gumroad is retrying. The money hasn't arrived, so we'd leave it out of the headline number and show it on its own line: `=SUMIFS(Members!D:D, Members!C:C, "pending_failure")` gives the at-risk amount per billing period.
- **Ended is ended.** `cancelled`, `failed_payment` and `fixed_subscription_period_ended` all contribute zero.
- **Pick a rule and stay consistent.** Changing it month to month makes your growth chart meaningless.

## Membership MRR next to one-time sales

Most Gumroad sellers have both. Keep two numbers side by side:

- **MRR** from the `Members` tab, as above.
- **Net revenue** for the month from your sales: one-time and membership charges, with refunded sales taken out.

MRR tells you what the base looks like. Net revenue tells you what actually landed. For getting every sale into a sheet, see [Gumroad sales spreadsheet: get your full history into Google Sheets](/blog/gumroad-sales-spreadsheet).
